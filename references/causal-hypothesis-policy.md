# Candidate Hypothesis Policy

Causal explanations are independent of the fact, event, and entity layers. They are not loaded every turn by default, and they are not generated merely because two terms co-occur.

## Triggers

- The user explicitly asks why something formed;
- Multiple independent experiences repeat the same condition–response–adaptation chain across time;
- The current decision requires comparing causes;
- A deep review finds several models depending on the same mechanism.

## Generation

Each hypothesis requires `claim`, `mechanism`, `supports`, `contradicts`, `alternatives`, `scope`, `confidence`, and `status: candidate`. Find at least two pieces of supporting evidence; when no counterexample exists, state "counterexample missing" explicitly — never pretend there are none.

## Invocation boundary

Ordinary fact or tool questions do not load causal hypotheses. For personal evaluation, choices, reflection, or "why am I like this" questions, survey the hypothesis catalog first, then probe/deep as needed. A hypothesis never substitutes for user confirmation and never turns into a diagnosis.

## Revision

User corrections have the highest priority. New evidence can weaken, qualify, or supersede a hypothesis, but the old hypothesis and its rationale are retained as history.
